# Abstract

**Title**: The Association between AI Technology Events and Job Insecurity Expression: An Empirical Analysis Based on Social Media Data

**Background**: The rapid development of artificial intelligence technology has attracted widespread social concern about job displacement. However, existing research on AI technology threats and job insecurity mostly relies on cross-sectional questionnaire surveys, lacking examination of public emotional dynamics in real-world contexts.

**Objective**: This study aims to analyze multi-source social media data to reveal how AI technology events affect public job insecurity expression, as well as the temporal dynamics, group differences, and long-term trends of such influence.

**Methods**: Using a mixed-methods approach integrating Job Demands-Resources (JD-R) model, Conservation of Resources (COR) theory, and Technology Acceptance Model (TAM), we conducted text mining and time series analysis on 114,915 comments from four social media platforms (Weibo, Zhihu, Bilibili, and Xiaohongshu) from October 2024 to March 2026. We constructed an event database containing 33 major AI technology events and employed event study methodology to examine emotional change trajectories before and after events.

**Results**: Our findings reveal: (1) **Significant main effect**: Following AI technology events, job insecurity expression increased significantly by 45%, indicating that regardless of event nature (technological breakthrough or product launch), all events trigger public concerns about job displacement; (2) **Rapid adaptation pattern**: Event effects showed rapid decay characteristics, with a half-life of approximately 1.36 days and return to baseline within 7-10 days, supporting the "rapid adaptation hypothesis"; (3) **Significant group differences**: Expression levels varied significantly across platforms, with Zhihu users showing the highest expression rate (2.80%), revealing a "high-knowledge anxiety paradox" where groups with deeper cognition express stronger anxiety; (4) **Structural concern trend**: After controlling for event effects, the baseline of job insecurity showed a significant upward trend during the study period, reflecting that public perception of AI threats shifted from "event-driven" to "structural concern".

**Theoretical Contributions**: This study integrates technology shock theory with job insecurity research, proposing a "technology event-emotional response" analytical framework. We reveal the temporal trajectory of emotional changes after technology shocks (shock-peak-decay-recovery), discover expression differences across social media communities, and challenge the unidimensional understanding of "digital divide", providing a new theoretical perspective for understanding public psychological responses in the context of technological change.

**Practical Implications**: Our findings provide empirical evidence for policymakers to identify triggers and time windows of public anxiety, for enterprises to conduct employee communication and technology upgrade management, and for educational institutions to design AI literacy and career transition guidance programs.

**Limitations**: The sample comes from in-depth discussion platforms, which may underestimate anxiety among less educated groups; rule-based sentiment analysis accuracy still has room for improvement; observational design cannot establish strict causal relationships; focusing on the Chinese context, the cross-cultural generalizability of conclusions needs further verification.

**Keywords**: Artificial Intelligence; Job Insecurity; Social Media Analysis; Time Series; Event Study; Emotional Evolution
